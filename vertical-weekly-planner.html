Vertical vs. columns: which layout wins?

Columns shine when your week is appointment-driven — you think in time slots and want the whole week visible like a calendar. Vertical shines when your week is task-driven — each day accumulates a list, and a wide row gives that room to breathe. Many people print both and use columns on the fridge as the family overview, vertical in a binder as the working plan.

FAQ

What is a vertical weekly planner?

It's a weekly planner where the seven days are stacked as rows down a portrait page, instead of side-by-side columns on a landscape page. Each day gets a long ruled row with plenty of horizontal writing space.

When should I pick vertical instead of day columns?

Pick vertical when your days are full: appointments, time blocks and long to-do lists fit much better in a wide row than in a narrow column. It also files neatly into a standard binder without rotating the page.

Does the vertical planner print on A4 and US Letter?

Yes. Choose your paper size in the panel above and the generator resizes the sheet exactly — the printed page fills A4 or US Letter edge to edge with zero margins.
